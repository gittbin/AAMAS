Programmable 6G networks combine heterogeneous control mechanisms that act on a shared physical environment but differ in their observations, action spaces, and update timescales. We study whether such components benefit from decentralized multi-agent control and explicit inter-agent communication under imperfect and delayed observations. We formulate the problem as a decentralized partially observable Markov decision process with three agents controlling transmit power, reconfigurable-surface phases, and movable-antenna layout. Separate recurrent MAPPO actors are trained under centralized training with decentralized execution, while communication is selective, graph-constrained, budget-limited, explicitly priced, and delayed by one step. Across five independent training seeds and paired evaluations, decentralized adaptation significantly improves worst-user fairness over strong static configurations, with its advantage increasing as channel uncertainty grows. However, a centralized recurrent controller and a non-learning online re-optimizer match or slightly exceed the multi-agent team in raw return; the learned agents recover about 98\% of the online optimizer's return at roughly two orders of magnitude lower per-step computational cost. Actuator ablations identify movable-antenna control as the main source of adaptation gains. Surprisingly, explicit communication provides no benefit under the current message design: recipients are insensitive to message corruption, communication remains cost-insensitive, and topology search favors empty or near-empty graphs. These results separate the value of decentralized adaptation from the value of communication and provide a reproducible testbed for studying heterogeneous coordination under delayed partial observability.
